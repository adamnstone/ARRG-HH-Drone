ARRG-HH-Drone: what is on gabriel and how to run it

Acoustic drone detector with 360° haptic bearing for the headband · 2026-10-08 · branch gabriel (head bd60f4b) · for the hackathon team

1. Summary

What it is. A headband that hears a multirotor drone and tells the wearer where it is by touch. Three Adafruit PDM MEMS mics (ST MP34DT01-M) on an ESP32-S3 sample sound in sync. A harmonic-comb detector and a small CNN decide whether a drone is present, and masked GCC-PHAT across the three mics gives a 360° bearing. Three coin motors (Left, Right, Back) vibrate toward the drone.

Measured so far (details and caveats in section 6)

  • Real phone clips: the fused detector (rules AND classifier) reaches DETECTED on all 9 committed drone recordings, first detection at 2.6–9.3 s. It never fires on the 237 s ambient recording. The thresholds were tuned on these same 9 clips, so 9/9 is optimistic.
  • False alarms, ESC-50 (all 2000 clips): fused 0.19 % of frames / 11 clips; rules alone 2.2 % of frames / 5.6 % of clips.
  • Classifier (int8, held-out split, p > 0.5): accuracy 94.9 %, recall 88.2 %, false-positive rate (FPR) 3.2 %.
  • Bearing (simulation only): 0.3–0.6° median error at 10 dB SNR. A drone circling the head over real ambient at 3 dB SNR is tracked with 4.3° median error.

2. Architecture: the per-frame pipeline

One frame is 2048 samples at 48 kHz (42.7 ms, 23.44 Hz bins), with a new frame every 1024 samples (21.3 ms, 46.9 frames/s). The Python (droneears/pipeline.py), the C++ core and the sketch all run the same loop.

Stage (file)What it does, with the numbers from README.md / droneears/config.py
1. Spectra
features.py		Each channel gets a periodic Hann window and an FFT. The mono power spectrum feeds the detector and the classifier; the three complex spectra feed the bearing. One FFT per channel per frame is the whole compute budget.
2. Harmonic detector
detector.py		Prominence = the dB spectrum minus a smooth baseline that ignores peaks (31 bins, about 730 Hz), averaged over frames with an EMA (0.5 weight on the new frame). The comb tries every f0 from 120 to 400 Hz in 2 Hz steps and scores the mean prominence of 5 consecutive harmonics, starting at the first one at or above 300 Hz. Hysteresis: a candidate within 6 % of the previous f0 is kept if it scores within 0.5 dB of the best. A frame counts as drone evidence when all of these hold:
  • score > 5.5 dB
  • at least 3 of the 8 harmonics are > 6 dB proud
  • spectral flatness in 300–3000 Hz is between 0.17 and 0.45 (this rejects pure tones and white noise)
  • f0 is steady: at least 35 % of the last ~1 s of estimates are within 6 % of their median, checked once 0.5 s of history exists
DETECTED needs 70 of the last 94 frames (~1.5 s of evidence in a 2 s window). It releases after 2 s with ≤ 20 frames.
3. Classifier gate
classifier.py, scripts/train.py		Input: a 47×40 log-mel window (1.0 s, 40 bands from 100 to 8000 Hz), normalised to the window maximum and clipped to −80 dB. Model: a CNN with 4 conv layers, global pool and dense (15 698 parameters, 24 KB int8), run twice a second. Fusion: a detection can only start if p > 0.7 on the latest update or on any update in the last ~1 s (47 frames).
4. Bearing
bearing.py		For each mic pair: cross-spectrum conj(Xi)·Xj, PHAT-normalised and masked to the bins at k·f0 (8 harmonics, ±1 bin), then an inverse FFT and a parabolic peak fit give the delay τ. Least squares turns the three delays into a direction: u = A+τ. |u| ≈ 1 is a free consistency check, with a minimum of 0.6. θ = atan2(ux, uy): 0° ahead, +90° right, ±180° behind. A circular EMA (α = 0.1) smooths θ while DETECTED. Mics: L (−0.10, 0), R (+0.10, 0), B (0, −0.12) m.
5. Haptics
bearing.py, sketch		The two motors bracketing θ share the vibration (L −90°, R +90°, B 180°); the pulse rate follows detection strength. Drone to the left buzzes L; back-left buzzes B and L; straight ahead buzzes L and R together (there is no front motor). Overhead buzzes all three at full weight. The mics are coplanar, so conf = |u| ≈ cos(elevation): overhead is declared when conf < 0.5 for 23 consecutive frames (~0.5 s) while DETECTED, and released when conf > 0.7 for 23 frames. θ stays frozen while overhead.

Every threshold and the mic geometry live in droneears/config.py. scripts/export_c_headers.py regenerates firmware/core/dsp_config.h from it, so the C core cannot drift from the Python.

4. Setup on a Mac

  1. ffmpeg (decodes the m4a/mp3 recordings): brew install ffmpeg
  2. Virtual environment and requirements: 
    cd ARRG-HH-Drone
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
    requirements.txt: numpy, scipy, soundfile, matplotlib, tensorflow ≥ 2.16 (training and TFLite), sounddevice (live_mac.py only), pytest, pyserial ≥ 3.5 (device scripts), pyarrow (DADS parquet). The examples below call .venv/bin/python directly; source .venv/bin/activate and then python works just as well.
  3. ESC-50 (needed by eval_esc50.py and train.py; optional otherwise): 
    git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/karolpiczak/ESC-50 data/ESC-50     # or set ESC50_DIR
  4. DADS public drone audio (optional; training skips it if it is missing): 
    .venv/bin/python scripts/get_dads.py                                # default: shards 0,38
.venv/bin/python scripts/get_dads.py --shards 0,5,12,20,28,36       # the 6 shards (~1.7 GB) behind the shipped model
    The files go to data/hf-drone/ (or DADS_DIR). The highest-numbered shard present becomes the test shard.
  5. A C++ compiler for compare_core.py and the C++ test: g++ from the Xcode command-line tools (xcode-select --install).
  6. Check: .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests should report 28 passed in about 20 s.
Run pytest on tests, not the whole repo. The README says pytest -q. Once ESC-50 is cloned into data/ESC-50, a bare pytest -q also collects data/ESC-50/tests/test_dataset.py, which imports librosa and stops collection with an error. Use pytest -q tests.

5.12 scripts/retune_holdout.py: retune thresholds on the held-out session

Purpose
Sweeps a grid of detector constants over the held-out drone and background clips and an ESC-50 sample. It recommends the config that detects the most drone clips, subject to two limits: no background clip may have any detection, and ESC-50 DETECTED frames must stay ≤ --max-esc-fa (0.5 % fused, 3 % rules-only). Ties are broken by lower ESC false alarms, then earlier median first detection, then fewer changes from the current config. For threshold-only grids the pipeline runs once per file and the state machine is replayed per config (checked against the real pipeline). Otherwise, or with --full, it reruns the pipeline per config in parallel. Constants that are consumed at import time (comb tables, frame geometry) are refused.
Options

6.4 Bearing (simulation only)

  • Static source, 10 dB SNR in white noise: 0.3–0.6° median error in all eight compass directions, including behind. At 0 dB: 0.8–2.2°.
  • Drone circling at 22.5°/s over the real ambient mixed in at 3 dB SNR: 4.3° median error, almost all of it EMA lag (the raw per-frame median is ~1°). At 0 dB over that ambient the detector does not fire, so there is no bearing.
  • The ambient is mixed with an independent offset per mic, which makes it spatially uncorrelated. That is easier than a real directional sound field.
Caveats. (1) The detector thresholds were tuned on the same 9 clips, and the margins are thin: Hovering 10 m peaks at 74 hits against 70 needed, and the ambient peaks at 63. (2) The team test windows come from the same recordings as the training windows (different time segments), so a whole-session holdout is still the real test. (3) All recordings so far are from a phone, not the MP34DT01-M PDM mics: expect domain shift (section 8). (4) The firmware has never been compiled or run on hardware; the bearing is untested outside simulation.

6.5 Expected real-world range (team estimate, not measured)

These figures are the team's expectation and have not been measured. Within ~30 m in quiet ambient, expect 80–90 % of flights to be detected within 3–5 s. Through the headband mics before a retrain, 50–80 %; after one field session and a retrain, 85–95 %. At ~50 m, 50–80 %; at 100 m, likely under 30 %. False alarms: about 0–2 per hour in quiet outdoor ambient, several per hour near mowers, chainsaws and vacuums. Bearing: typically 5–20° when the drone is loud; untested on hardware.

7. Firmware (firmware/esp32s3/headband.ino)

Status: written against the ESP-IDF 5.x headers and docs and the mic datasheet; not yet compiled on hardware. The board is an ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 on the Arduino-ESP32 3.x core (ESP-IDF 5.1–5.5 underneath). From the DSP core down, it is the same code that passes compare_core.py.

7.1 What the sketch does

Task (core)Job
dsp_task (core 0, prio 5)PDM capture: I2S0 in PDM RX mode, 2 data lines, one 3.072 MHz clock, so all three channels are sample-aligned by construction. Reads 1024-sample chunks and runs spectra, log-mel, detector and bearing every chunk. At boot it prints # pdm chunk_us=… (expect 21333).
ml_task (core 1, prio 2)Classifier every 500 ms (TFLM, 60 KB arena) when USE_TFLM 1. With USE_TFLM 0 (the current default), p = 1 and only the harmonic path decides.
alert_task (core 1, prio 3)Buzzer chirp at detection onset. Motor PWM (200 Hz, 8-bit, duty capped at 0.6 for 3 V motors on the 5 V rail); pulse rate 1–5 Hz from strength. LED on while DETECTED, blinking otherwise.
telemetry_task (core 1, prio 1)The only writer to Serial. In telemetry mode it sends one CSV T line per frame; in raw mode it sends binary 3-channel audio packets; it reads single-byte host commands (section 7.5).

Mics: Adafruit PDM MEMS breakouts (product 3492 / 4346, ST MP34DT01-M, 3.3 V, PDM clock 1–3.25 MHz). Geometry: L (−0.10, 0), R (+0.10, 0), B (0, −0.12) m. 7.3 Build notes

  • Arduino IDE or arduino-cli with the Arduino-ESP32 3.x core; board ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1. Open firmware/esp32s3/ as the sketch folder. src/ holds symlinks to ../core; on a checkout without symlinks, copy the three files in instead.
  • USB CDC on boot: enabled, so that Serial is the native USB port. Telemetry and raw-audio streaming go over it at 921600 baud.
  • Classifier: set #define USE_TFLM 1 and install esp-tflite-micro (Espressif's component) or the TensorFlowLite_ESP32 Arduino library. The sketch includes model_data.h from the sketch folder.
  • Optional ESP-DSP: replace dsp::fft_* with dsps_fft2r_fc32 (about 5× faster; ~1.5 ms of the 21 ms frame budget).
  • RAM (README): dsp_core.cpp static arrays 57 KB, plus the sketch's objects 80 KB, plus its buffers 69 KB (including the 25 KB raw-audio stream ring), plus the 60 KB TFLM arena when enabled.

7.4 Bench checks, in this order

  1. Slot / SEL mapping. Tap each mic in turn with a fingernail and watch which channel spikes. If L/R/B land in the wrong place, fix CH_SLOT[]; no other code changes. Also confirm the boot line chunk_us is ~21333 µs. This validates the assumed 4-slot DMA frame; ~16000 would mean 3 slots.
  2. DC offset per channel. Gain does not matter (the classifier normalises each window), but the S3 PDM RX has no hardware high-pass filter, so check each channel's mean.
  3. Clean audio at 3.072 MHz. This is above the Adafruit guide's 1–3 MHz but inside the MP34DT01-M's 3.25 MHz maximum, and it comes from a fractional divider of the 160 MHz PLL. Record a few seconds with scripts/record_device.py and listen to it and look at its spectrum, checking for tones or crackle.

Device → host, rawOne packet per chunk, little endian: A5 5A, then uint16 n_frames (= 1024), uint32 chunk_index, then 1024 × 3 int16 interleaved L,R,B (6152 bytes). A gap in chunk_index means chunks were dropped; the DSP never waits on Serial. Raw mode streams ~288 KB/s, which needs the S3's native USB port with USB CDC On Boot enabled. 8. Device bring-up and field session

From README.md ("Device bring-up, plug and play"), TRAINING_PLAN.md sections 4–5 and the help text of the three host tools. None of this has been run against a real board yet.

8.1 Bring-up

  1. Wire the three mics and the motors as in 7.2.
  2. Flash with Arduino-ESP32 3.x (plus esp-tflite-micro if USE_TFLM 1). Use the native USB port with USB CDC On Boot: Enabled.
  3. Watch it run: scripts/telemetry.py --log run1.csv. You should see ~47 lines/s and # pdm chunk_us= close to 21333. Plot the log afterwards with --plot run1.csv --out run1.png.
  4. Tap test: run scripts/record_device.py --monitor and tap each mic in turn. The L, R and B columns should jump in that order; if they don't, fix CH_SLOT[] in the sketch. The same display shows DC offset and clipping (bench checks 7.4, steps 1–2).
  5. Audio quality: record ~10 s (record_device.py --seconds 10 --out /tmp/bench.wav), then listen and look at the spectrum for tones or crackle (check 3). run_file.py /tmp/bench.wav also gives the bearing, because the file has 3 channels.
  6. Haptics: walk a phone playing a drone clip around the head. Check L, B+L, B, B+R, R and L+R for ahead, then hold it overhead and check that all three buzz. The hap_* columns in the telemetry log show the same weights.

8.3 Retune and retrain

  1. The recordings land in data/team/drone/ and data/team/background/ (or copy them there).
  2. Thresholds: run .venv/bin/python scripts/retune_holdout.py. Read the table: drone clips detected, background clips with any detection (must be 0) and ESC-50 frames. Then --apply writes the winner into config.py and regenerates dsp_config.h. To tune by hand instead, compare the score distributions that run_file.py prints for a drone file and a background file, and put HARM_THRESH_DB / ML_THRESH in the gap. Tune on the held-out session, never on training data.
  3. Classifier (if it is what fails): retrain from scratch with scripts/train.py --epochs 30 --n-synth 60. With real positives present, synthetic clips stay a minority. In train_report.json, team:drone recall is the headline number, and the team:background FPR (now including our motors) is the number that decides the demo.
  4. scripts/export_c_headers.py, copy artifacts/model_data.h into firmware/esp32s3/, reflash. Budget: about 20 minutes of compute, an hour including labelling.

8.4 What to watch for (TRAINING_PLAN section 5)

  • Domain shift (phone vs MP34DT01-M frequency response and noise) is the main failure mode. Window normalisation removes gain differences but not differences in spectral shape.
  • Far drones are faint and low-passed by the air, so check recall on the *_100m* files.
  • Over-trusting accuracy: keep the splits by source. A random window split would report 99 % and mean nothing.
